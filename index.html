Australian accommodation, 2018–2026

How evenly has accommodation demand recovered?

Australia’s accommodation recovery varied by type, destination and season. Explore how domestic and international demand returned unevenly. Then compare rental prices and seasonal hotel occupancy.

Section 1

Uneven recovery in domestic accommodation

Recovery differed across domestic accommodation types.

Hotels recovered after the sharpest fall

Hotel nights nearly halved, reaching 51.6 million in 2020. By 2024, nights reached 113.5 million, exceeding 2019 by 12.4% hotels passed their 2019 visitor-night level in 2022.

Three categories still lagged behind 2019

Rented stays reached 34.7 million nights, 2.7% below 2019. Camping recorded 34.7 million nights, 4.7% below 2019. Friends or relatives recorded 133.2 million nights, down 7.0%.

Friends’ stays remained the largest category

Friends or relatives recorded 133.2 million nights in 2024. That was 33.5% of all domestic visitor nights. Their total still exceeded hotels’ 113.5 million nights.

Hotels gained share during an incomplete recovery

Hotel share rose from 24.2% to 28.6% since 2019. All domestic nights remained 4.9% below 2019. The 397.5 million nights fell short of 2019’s 417.9 million.

Section 2

Domestic demand varied by location and quarter

Demand concentrated in New South Wales, Queensland and Victoria. Peak quarters differed across the states and territories.

Three states dominated domestic visitor nights

New South Wales led in every quarter shown. It recorded 27.7 million nights in June 2026. With Queensland and Victoria, it contributed 78.1% of national nights.

Peak quarters differed between states

March 2026 led in six of the eight jurisdictions. Queensland and the Northern Territory peaked in September 2025. Northern Territory’s September demand was 2.4 times its March demand.

Rankings conceal changes in demand

Sydney regained first place with 13.43 million nights in 2024. Melbourne followed with 13.11 million, just 324,488 nights behind. Brisbane reached 8.25 million nights, up 30.8% since 2019. Its third-place finish matched 2019, despite higher demand.

Hotel share differed from hotel volume

Queensland’s 31.9% hotel share exceeded New South Wales’ 28.9%. New South Wales still recorded 33.0 million hotel nights. Queensland followed with 31.5 million, despite its higher share. Friends’ stays led four states, showing demand beyond commercial hotels.

Section 3

International recovery varied across markets and stays

International recovery varied across visitor markets, states and accommodation types.

Across these eight markets, trips rose 8.3% to 5.36 million, with seven markets recording growth.

Singapore grew 7.9%, while India declined 3.5%, suggesting uneven potential accommodation demand across nearby visitor markets.

Growth and volume told different stories

Queensland grew 19.4%, reaching 62.2 million visitor nights; New South Wales’ 106.6 million remained largest, despite 4.7% growth. The Australian Capital Territory fell 17.3%, the largest decline among the eight states and territories.

Rented homes outpaced hotel growth

Rented homes gained 18.3 million nights, reaching 144.0 million, while hotels rose 7.7% to 34.4 million nights. Stays with friends or relatives fell 8.6 million, showing that recovery varied by the type of stay.

Section 4

Rental supply, prices and seasonal hotel occupancy

Compare rental supply, advertised prices and seasonal hotel occupancy.

Melbourne had more recorded listings

Recorded listings: Melbourne 25,728; Sydney 20,573. Melbourne’s largest area held 32.6%, versus Sydney’s 24.4%. Listings show supply, rather than bookings or occupancy.

Room type mattered more than city

Entire home medians: Sydney $326; Melbourne $276. Private room medians: Sydney $112; Melbourne $108. Hotel samples: Sydney 51; Melbourne 39. Compare cautiously.

Occupancy recovered, but remained below baseline

National occupancy rose from 49.6% in 2020–2021 to 72.0% in 2024–2025, recovering 22.4 percentage points. However, it remained below 2018–2019’s 73.8%, showing recovery in room occupancy was still incomplete.

Seasonal peaks differed between states

In 2024–2025, Tasmania peaked in February at 87.0%, while the Northern Territory peaked in July at 76.8%. The contrasting peaks show that one national seasonal pattern cannot describe every state and territory.

Final findings

Conclusion

Accommodation recovered unevenly across types, places and seasons.

Recovery returned at different speeds

Hotels led the domestic rebound

In 2024, hotel nights stood 12.4% above 2019. All domestic visitor nights remained 4.9% below 2019.

Demand varied by location and accommodation

Three states supplied 78.1% of domestic nights in June 2026. International rented homes gained 18.3 million nights during 2025–2026.

Occupancy remained below its earlier baseline

National occupancy reached 72.0% in 2024–2025. Its 2018–2019 baseline was 73.8%; seasonal peaks differed.

Rental supply and prices differed

Melbourne recorded 25,728 listings, compared with Sydney’s 20,573. Sydney’s median home price exceeded Melbourne’s in this dataset. These advertised listings do not measure bookings or occupancy.
