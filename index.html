Australian accommodation, 2018–2026

How evenly has accommodation demand recovered?

Australia’s accommodation recovery varied across accommodation types, destinations and visitor markets. Explore where visitor nights returned, how accommodation preferences differed and when hotels were busiest.

Section 1

How did accommodation demand recover?

Domestic accommodation followed different recovery paths. By 2024, hotel visitor nights exceeded their 2019 level, while the other three categories shown remained below it.

Read the recovery: each category starts at 100 in 2019. Values above the dashed line indicate more visitor nights than in 2019; values below it indicate fewer. These are visitor nights, not hotel occupancy rates.

Comparability: this recovery comparison ends in 2024. The National Visitor Survey ended in December 2024. Domestic Tourism Statistics began in 2025 using a different collection method, so the two series are not joined here.

Band thickness represents domestic visitor nights, rather than vertical position. “Other” combines own property, guest houses, bed and breakfasts and miscellaneous accommodation. Values are shown in millions of visitor nights.

Section 2

Where was domestic demand concentrated?

National totals conceal geographic differences. Compare quarterly demand, changes in destination rankings and the accommodation mix across states.

Darker cells represent more domestic visitor nights. Displayed values allow comparisons without relying on colour alone.

Rank 1 represents the destination with the most domestic visitor nights in hotels and similar accommodation among the destinations shown. Crossings indicate changes in rank. Hover over a point to see the visitor-night value.

Column width represents each state’s total domestic visitor nights. Segment height represents an accommodation type’s share within that state, so segment area represents its visitor-night volume. The five largest states are shown.

Section 3

How did international travel return?

International travel changed unevenly across visitor markets, Australian states and accommodation types. These charts compare the years ending June 2025 and June 2026.

Thicker routes represent more international trips to Australia. Blue origin points indicate growth from 2025, while orange indicates a decline. Values represent thousands of trips.

Colour represents the percentage change in international visitor nights between the years ending June 2025 and June 2026. It shows relative growth or decline, rather than the total number of visitor nights.

Grey points show 2025 and blue points show 2026. Labels show millions of international visitor nights. The distance between the points indicates the change for each accommodation category.

Section 4

How do supply, prices and seasons shape the industry?

Compare advertised short-term-rental supply and prices in Sydney and Melbourne, then explore seasonal patterns in commercial accommodation occupancy.

Each circle represents a neighbourhood. Larger circles contain more short-term-rental listings, while darker circles have higher median advertised nightly prices.

Curves show the distribution of advertised nightly prices. Listings above AUD 1,000 are excluded from the displayed distributions for readability. Dashed lines show category medians. Advertised prices are not confirmed booking prices.

Each ring represents one state or territory and each segment represents one month, beginning with July. Darker cells indicate higher commercial accommodation occupancy. The selected years show the pre-pandemic baseline, pandemic disruption, recovery and latest available period.
